A page can take its time.

A sketchbook holds experiments, corrections and things worth returning to. Start with one subject and a few tools. Let what you see determine the next marks.

Review exhibition studies ↗Explore the tools ↗Inspect a saved study ↗See the fresh-agent trial ↗
  1. Choose a medium. The source atelier works with repeatable native stroke programs. The material studio works with pressure, tilt, graphite and charcoal. Try a few marks before asking the tools to carry a finished piece.
  2. Look at two scales. At fit view, assess the composition. In a detail, assess edges, overlaps and material. Name a specific problem and choose the smallest useful correction.
  3. Continue or branch deliberately. Continue keeps the previous pigment and adds marks. A new version can change the construction. Both retain their earlier saved checkpoints.
  4. Compare, then decide. Each pass can retain what you saw, what you changed and what to inspect next. Stop when further marks have no clear purpose. A saved study need not become exhibition work.

From an observed problem to a correction

The near aperture initially reads as a flat disk; the supports have little weight. Later passes distinguish the rim, glass and mounting hardware. A separate branch corrects a scale error in the tiny screws. The study still has open questions about its clean silhouette.

The agent handoff includes exact commands, resuming a checkpoint, and the distinction between saving and selecting. Notes record experience for an agent to read later; they do not train the model. The drawing remains native mark data throughout.
